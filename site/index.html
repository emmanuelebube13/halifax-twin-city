Overview

One map that answers one question at a time

Halifax publishes useful data, but it sits in separate portals, formats and update cycles with nothing connecting it. Cross-sector questions, such as whether new housing is being built where transit is weakest, are slow or impossible to answer.

The twin collects open data into its own PostgreSQL and PostGIS warehouse and serves it to one web map for laptop and phone. Each answered question is a story: the question, a one-sentence answer with its number, the map layers that prove it, context, and where the data came from, with its age and reliability.

The city is shown in 2D always, and in realistic 3D where available. The map reads only a small, read-only serving API, so new sectors add layers, not rewrites.

An independent personal project by Emmanuel Mbachu, not affiliated with or endorsed by Halifax Regional Municipality. Everything runs on local or free tools.

Guiding principles

  1. Provenance on every record: source, load time and a reliability tier.
  2. Finest grain first: store each bus position or permit, summarise upward.
  3. Real-time and history together: nothing is overwritten.
  4. Additive change: new sectors and sources are added beside what exists.
  5. Small blast radius: each source has its own pipeline.
  6. Privacy and licence compliance: no personal data in Phase 1.
  7. Open by default, private by choice.
  8. One application, many layers.
  9. API-first.

Phase 1 sectors

Three sectors on the waterfront first

Seventeen sectors were scored on data availability (weighted twice), value and links to other sectors. These three scored highest.

Transportation

28questions, TRN-Q01 to Q28

Buses in real time against the schedule, late routes, bunching, missed trips, slow segments and road closures.

  • Halifax Transit live feeds and schedule
  • Road closures and traffic

Public Works

39questions, PWK-Q01 to Q39

Utility work and planned closures that restrict streets, and the effect on buses, traffic and walking.

  • Right-of-way and street closure permits
  • Capital projects and works

Housing and real estate

33questions, HRE-Q01 to Q33

Building permits, new rental units and how long development takes, where it happens and at what scale.

  • Building permits
  • Rental and housing starts

All three share one core: Location, Time, Party, Asset and Event. A bus, a permit and a road closure are each an event at a place and a time, which is what lets one question cross sectors.

Data model

From raw feed to answer, in three layers

Logical Model V1 has 63 entities and 371 attributes in third normal form. Physical Model V1 builds it in PostgreSQL with PostGIS.

Bronzeschema bronze

Raw files exactly as each source sent them, kept on local disk for a rolling seven days, with one row per download in a fetch register. A gap view over the register is the evidence that the archive has no holes.

Silverschema silver

The logical model in third normal form, 63 tables. History is kept as slowly changing dimensions, Type 2, on reference data and the location, party and asset families, so nothing is overwritten. Vehicle positions are partitioned by month.

Goldschema gold

Star schemas shaped for questions and dashboards, built as each history story needs them. The serving API reads Silver and Gold; the map never queries the warehouse directly.

The shared core

Every sector joins to these. Kernel entities carry a surrogate key, with the source's own key kept as an alternate key.

Location

Where: a point, line, area, service area or the whole city. Streets, street segments, intersections, areas, civic addresses and parcels are its subtypes.

geometry, EPSG 4326
Time

When: a calendar from 2015 to 2035 with Nova Scotia holidays, and UTC timestamps throughout.

timestamptz
Party

Who: organizations such as Halifax Transit and the municipality. No personal data.

organization
Asset

What exists: routes, stops, vehicles, route segments, infrastructure, facilities, buildings and dwelling units.

8 subtypes
Event

What happened: a trip, a stop arrival, a service alert, a road closure, a building permit. Twenty-six subtypes.

26 subtypes
